Reaching the right person, safely.

Project PULSE is a student wellbeing, support and safety initiative being developed under the Tyler Nicholas Foundation.

The project aims to create a secure digital pathway through which students can access wellbeing support, communicate with authorised support professionals, raise concerns and receive appropriate assistance directly through the digital systems they already use for education.

Rather than creating another completely separate platform for students to manage, Project PULSE is being developed around the possibility of integration into Seychelles' national OpenEMIS education platform.

The goal is simple: make it easier for a student who needs support to reach the right person safely, privately and efficiently.

The problem

Asking for help should not be difficult.

Students may experience stress, academic pressure, bullying, personal challenges, safety concerns or other difficulties during their education. However, seeking support is not always easy.

Some students may not know who to approach. Others may feel uncomfortable visiting a counselling office publicly, may worry about confidentiality, or may simply not know what support is available.

At the same time, schools need better ways to understand broader wellbeing patterns and determine where additional support, resources or counselling capacity may be needed.

Project PULSE aims to help bridge that gap.

The PULSE solution

One secure pathway to student support.

Project PULSE proposes a dedicated wellbeing and student-support environment integrated into the national education digital system.

A student could access PULSE from their normal student account and use it to check in on their wellbeing, request support, contact an authorised counsellor, report a concern or access trusted information.

The system would also help authorised school wellbeing teams organise support requests and follow-ups more efficiently.

At a broader level, anonymised and aggregated information could help schools and education authorities better understand student wellbeing trends without unnecessarily exposing individual students' confidential information.

What PULSE could offer

Six core features, designed around what students need and what support teams can act on.

Wellbeing check-ins

Short and optional student check-ins designed to identify general wellbeing, stress and school-pressure trends.

Request support

A simple and discreet way for students to ask to speak with an authorised counsellor or wellbeing professional.

Counsellor access

Students could request appointments or begin contact with authorised support personnel digitally.

Report a concern

A structured pathway for appropriate bullying, wellbeing and student-safety concerns.

Support resources

Trusted information explaining where students can find help and what different support services provide.

Wellbeing insights

Anonymised trends could support better decisions about counselling capacity, student support and wellbeing initiatives.

How PULSE could work

From signing in to follow-up, in four steps.

  1. 01

    Student accesses PULSE

    The student signs into their normal education account and opens the Project PULSE section.

  2. 02

    Student chooses what they need

    They can complete a wellbeing check-in, request support, access resources or raise an appropriate concern.

  3. 03

    The right support team receives it

    Requests are routed according to the type of support required and the access permissions established by the responsible authorities.

  4. 04

    Support and follow-up

    Students can receive appropriate follow-up while authorised teams manage requests and broader anonymised trends.

OpenEMIS

Designed with OpenEMIS integration in mind.

The Seychelles Ministry of Education is developing its national education digital infrastructure using OpenEMIS, an education management information system used internationally.

Project PULSE is being designed around the possibility of becoming an integrated student wellbeing and support component within that environment rather than operating as an unrelated standalone platform.

This could allow PULSE to work alongside existing student identities, schools, authorised user roles and education information while introducing dedicated wellbeing and support functionality.

The exact technical implementation would ultimately need to be developed together with the responsible Ministry and OpenEMIS technical teams.

Built around trust.

Student trust must be at the centre of Project PULSE. If students do not trust it, it cannot help them.

  • Confidential by design

    The project is being designed around principles of confidentiality, appropriate role-based access, safeguarding and responsible use of information.

  • Only the people who need it

    Private student wellbeing information should only be available to authorised people who genuinely require access to provide support or meet safeguarding responsibilities.

  • No automatic teacher access

    Teachers should not automatically receive access to confidential wellbeing information simply because they teach a student.

  • Trends, not conversations

    National-level analysis should generally focus on anonymised and aggregated trends rather than unrestricted access to individual counselling conversations.

Project PULSE is intended as a support system, not a disciplinary or surveillance system.

Student consultation

PULSE should be built with students, not simply for them.

Before Project PULSE moves further into development, student feedback is essential.

The Project PULSE Student Consultation is designed to understand what students would actually find useful, what would make them trust the platform, which features matter most and what concerns they may have about privacy and confidentiality.

Responses are anonymous and will help guide the development of the project.

Stakeholder consultation

Help shape Project PULSE.

Project PULSE also needs input from those who may be involved in supporting, implementing or working alongside the system.

Why PULSE was created

Project PULSE is a student wellbeing, support and safety initiative developed as a flagship project under the Tyler Nicholas Foundation. The project originated as a post-program action project following participation in the Youth Ambassadors programme and is being developed with the long-term objective of improving how students in Seychelles access wellbeing support within the education system.

Students can experience a wide range of challenges during their education. These may include academic pressure, stress, bullying, interpersonal difficulties, concerns about safety, problems at home or simply periods when they feel overwhelmed and need somebody to speak with.

While schools already provide different forms of student support, students do not always feel comfortable accessing those services directly. A student may hesitate to physically approach a counselling office, may not know who is responsible for a particular issue or may worry about who will know that they have asked for help.

Project PULSE seeks to address these barriers by exploring how student wellbeing and support services could be made more accessible through the digital infrastructure students already use.

The proposed model would allow a student to enter a dedicated PULSE environment from their education account. Within that environment, students could potentially complete short wellbeing check-ins, request assistance, contact authorised counsellors, request appointments, raise bullying or safety concerns and access trusted wellbeing and support resources.

The project is not intended to replace counsellors, teachers, health professionals or safeguarding personnel. Technology would instead provide a pathway connecting students with the people and services capable of supporting them. Human review, professional judgement and appropriate safeguarding procedures would remain essential.

Project PULSE is also intended to help schools understand broader student wellbeing needs. When appropriate privacy safeguards are applied, aggregated and anonymised information may help identify trends such as increasing student stress, periods of high academic pressure, increased demand for counselling or patterns in reported bullying concerns. Such information could help schools and education authorities make better-informed decisions about wellbeing programmes, counsellor capacity, staffing and resources.

PULSE and OpenEMIS

Project PULSE is currently being developed around the possibility of integration into Seychelles' OpenEMIS-based national education platform.

OpenEMIS is an education management information system designed to support governments and education systems in managing student, school and education information.

Rather than creating another independent account, application and database for students, PULSE could potentially operate as a dedicated module or connected service within the existing national education infrastructure. A student could therefore access academic services and wellbeing support through the same wider digital environment.

The final technical architecture has not yet been determined and would require consultation and development with the relevant Ministry of Education and technical teams.

Our mission

To make student wellbeing support more accessible, confidential and responsive by connecting students with appropriate support through secure digital tools integrated into the education environment.

Our vision

A Seychelles education system where every student knows where to seek support, feels able to ask for help and can access appropriate assistance without unnecessary barriers.

Objectives

  • Improve student access to wellbeing and counselling support.
  • Reduce barriers that prevent students from asking for help.
  • Strengthen communication between students and authorised support professionals.
  • Provide safer pathways for raising wellbeing, bullying and safety concerns.
  • Support schools in understanding broader student wellbeing needs.
  • Provide useful anonymised information that can support better education planning.
  • Protect confidentiality and student trust.
  • Ensure students participate in shaping the system intended to support them.

Who PULSE is for

Students

A discreet, familiar place to check in, ask for help, reach a counsellor or raise a concern.

Counsellors and wellbeing teams

A clearer way to receive, organise and follow up on support requests.

Schools and education authorities

Anonymised, aggregated insight to plan wellbeing programmes, staffing and resources.

Privacy and confidentiality principles

Project PULSE is not intended to become a student surveillance system. Confidentiality and trust are fundamental to the concept.

Information should only be accessible according to appropriate roles and responsibilities. Counsellors and authorised wellbeing or safeguarding personnel may require access to certain individual information in order to provide support, while school administrators or national education authorities may require only broader anonymised information. Individual counselling conversations should not simply become general administrative records available to anyone within an education system.

  • Confidentiality
  • Safeguarding
  • Role-based access
  • Responsible data use
  • Transparency
  • Non-punitive support

Students should understand what information is collected, why it is collected and who may access it. Seeking wellbeing support should not negatively affect a student's academic record or automatically become a disciplinary matter.
